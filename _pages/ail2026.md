## Proposing a Classification Scheme for AI Usage in Scientific Articles

**TL;DR:** A short, human-readable tag that tells readers where AI was used in a paper, and how much, at a glance. Example:

`AI-0: Text1 Check2`: human research, AI-polished text, AI-checked computations.

### Why
- AI disclosure today is mostly a yes/no box or free text: it can't distinguish a spell check from an AI-generated proof, and it can't be searched or compared.
- Light AI use, such as proofreading, fixing references, or double-checking computations, **improves papers** and should be encouraged, not stigmatized.
- Essential AI contributions to the research itself (conjectures, theorems, proofs) should be **disclosed out of fairness**.

### How it works
- **Ten labels:** `Idea`, `Method`, `Result`, `Proof` (the research itself) and `Text`, `Lit`, `Code`, `Data`, `Figs`, `Check` (supporting work).
  - `Idea`: research questions, hypotheses, conjectures
  - `Method`: approach, study design, choice of model, proof strategy
  - `Result`: formulation of the main results (their statement, sharpening, or generalization)
  - `Proof`: justification of the results (proofs, derivations, analysis and interpretation of evidence)
  - `Text`: prose, language, structure, LaTeX; `Lit`: literature search, citations; `Code`: software, simulations, computations; `Data`: collection, cleaning, synthetic data; `Figs`: plots, diagrams, tables; `Check`: verification of computations, proofs, code, references
- **Depths 0–4:** from *none* through *polishing*, *directed*, and *substantive* to *autonomous*. Labels at depth 0 are omitted.
- **One headline level `AI-0` to `AI-4`:** summarizes AI's role in the research itself. `AI-3` and above mean AI contributed essential parts.
- AI verification (`Check`) never raises the headline: it is always encouraged.
